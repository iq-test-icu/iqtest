Methodology & Technical Notes

At IQ·Test, we believe in radical transparency. The score you receive at the end of our 16-question quiz is calculated by evaluating your logical reasoning, numerical recognition, spatial reasoning, and verbal comprehension. This document explains how our quiz is structured, how scores are calculated into a cognitive index, and what conclusions can and cannot be drawn from the results.

Categories measured

The quiz consists of 16 puzzle items divided equally into four core reasoning domains. Each item maps to exactly one category:

  • Numeric Reasoning: Evaluates your ability to spot patterns, structural intervals, and relationships in numerical sequences.
  • Verbal Reasoning: Assesses semantic relationships, comparative definitions, and analytical analogies.
  • Logic: Tests your ability to deduce valid conclusions from structured premises and statement relationships.
  • Pattern Recognition: Evaluates your speed at isolating visual consistency, sequential letter patterns, and spatial transformations.

Raw score to cognitive index mapping

On IQ·Test, each correct response scores 1 point, resulting in a raw score range from 0 to 16. This raw score is mapped to an overall Cognitive Index between 85 and 145. This mapping uses a deviation IQ model calibrated against our platform's own performance statistics. It compares an individual's raw performance score to the distribution of scores achieved by our self-selected user base, mapping it onto a standard normal distribution.

Percentile framing

Once the deviation Z-score is computed, it can be mapped directly to a percentile rank. The percentile rank indicates the percentage of the population that scores at or below that particular score level. For example:

  • A score of 100 places the individual in the 50th percentile (exactly average).
  • A score of 115 places them in approximately the 84th percentile.
  • A score of 130 places them in approximately the 98th percentile.
  • A score of 85 places them in approximately the 16th percentile.

Why the scale is a familiar reference, not diagnosis

This experience is designed as a puzzle-based cognitive self-insight quiz for entertainment and personal reflection. We use the 85 to 145 index purely as a familiar scaling metric. It is not a clinical, diagnostic, educational-placement, or employment-evaluation tool. Because our user base is self-selected rather than a clinically controlled national norm sample, the comparative percentile estimates represent standing relative to other test-takers on this platform.

How historical figure matching works

Our Detailed Reflection matches your strongest domain score with a thematic historical figure (such as Ada Lovelace, Mary Shelley, Aristotle, or Leonardo da Vinci). This pairing is purely a conceptual storytelling device based on historical style, not a claim of measured IQ equivalence, as verified test-score data does not exist for historical figures.

Data collection and limitations

Performance on online cognitive puzzles can fluctuate significantly based on multiple session-specific variables, such as sleep, physical fatigue, stress levels, and environmental distractions. Results are snapshots of performance on these specific tasks in one sitting.

We respect your privacy and do not sell your personal data. We store your email to deliver reports and anonymize session statistics after 90 days.

Frequently Asked Questions
