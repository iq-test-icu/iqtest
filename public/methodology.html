Methodology & Technical Notes

At IQ·Test, we believe in radical transparency. Below is an honest, plain-language description of how our quiz is structured, how scores are calculated, and what conclusions can and cannot be drawn from the results.

1. Objective of the Quiz

This experience is designed as a puzzle-based cognitive self-insight quiz for entertainment and personal reflection. It is intended to explore how you approach four core domains of logical reasoning. It is not a clinical, diagnostic, educational-placement, or employment-evaluation tool. If you require a validated, psychologist-administered intelligence scale (such as the WAIS or Stanford-Binet), please consult a licensed professional.

2. Structure & Construct Clarity

The quiz consists of 16 puzzle items divided equally into four core reasoning domains. Each item maps to exactly one category:

  • Numeric Reasoning: Evaluates your ability to spot patterns, structural intervals, and relationships in numerical sequences.
  • Verbal Reasoning: Assesses semantic relationships, comparative definitions, and analytical analogies.
  • Logic: Tests your ability to deduce valid conclusions from structured premises and statement relationships.
  • Pattern Recognition: Evaluates your speed at isolating visual consistency, sequential letter patterns, and spatial transformations.

3. Scoring Mechanism & Reference Scale (How IQ is Calculated)

To understand how a score is calculated on IQ·Test, it is helpful to look at how modern psychometric instruments compute intelligence scores. Historically, early tests calculated IQ using a simple ratio formula based on a subject's mental age relative to their chronological age. However, this ratio model had significant limitations (for example, mental age slows down in adulthood while chronological age continues to increase, causing scores to artificially drop with age).

The Deviation IQ Method

Modern intelligence scales (including the Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale and the Stanford-Binet) use the Deviation IQ method. This statistical approach calculates IQ by comparing an individual's raw performance score to the distribution of scores achieved by a representative peer group sample of their chronological age. This comparison is mapped onto a standard normal distribution (the bell curve).

Under this statistical framework, the population average is set to exactly 100, with the standard deviation (SD, denoted as σ) set to 15. The formula used to calculate a deviation IQ score is:

IQ = 100 + 15 * Z

Where Z represents the standard score or Z-score. The Z-score measures how many standard deviations an individual's raw score is away from the peer group mean. It is calculated using the formula:

Z = (X - μ) / σ

Where:

  • X: The individual's raw score (on our quiz, the number of correct answers out of 16).
  • μ (Mu): The average raw score of the peer standardization sample.
  • σ (Sigma): The standard deviation of the peer standardization sample's raw scores.

From Z-Scores to Percentiles

Once the Z-score is computed, it can be mapped directly to a percentile rank. The percentile rank indicates the percentage of the population that scores at or below that particular score level. For example:

  • A Z-score of 0 maps to an IQ of 100, placing the individual in the 50th percentile (exactly average).
  • A Z-score of +1.0 maps to an IQ of 115, placing them in approximately the 84th percentile.
  • A Z-score of +2.0 maps to an IQ of 130, placing them in approximately the 98th percentile.
  • A Z-score of -1.0 maps to an IQ of 85, placing them in approximately the 16th percentile.

Platform Scaling & Standardization

On IQ·Test, each correct response scores 1 point, resulting in a raw score range from 0 to 16. This raw score is mapped to an overall Cognitive Index between 85 and 145. This mapping uses the deviation IQ model calibrated against platform performance statistics. Because our user base is self-selected rather than a clinically controlled national norm sample, the comparative percentile estimates represent standing relative to other test-takers on this platform.

4. Caveats & Interpretation

Performance on online cognitive puzzles can fluctuate significantly based on multiple session-specific variables. These include:

  • Sleep and physical fatigue.
  • Stress levels and temporal focus.
  • Environmental distractions or noise.
  • The device screen size and interface responsiveness.

Because cognitive reasoning is dynamic, a single sitting on a 16-question puzzle set is not a permanent measure of capability. Results are snapshots of performance on these specific tasks.

5. Thematic Historical Comparison

Our Detailed Reflection matches your strongest domain score with a thematic historical figure (such as Ada Lovelace, Mary Shelley, Aristotle, or Leonardo da Vinci). This pairing is purely a conceptual storytelling device based on historical style, not a claim of measured IQ equivalence, as verified test-score data does not exist for historical figures.

6. Data Deletion & Privacy

We respect your privacy and do not sell your personal data. We store your email to deliver reports and anonymize session statistics after 90 days. You can request the complete and immediate deletion of your data and email at any time by writing to privacy@iq-test.icu.

To ensure high quality, we periodically review our item pool and scoring calibrations. Updates to the scoring model will be noted on this page with updated methodology versions.
