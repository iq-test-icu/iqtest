What is an IQ Test?

An Intelligence Quotient (IQ) test is a standardized psychometric assessment designed to measure human cognitive abilities and intellectual potential. Rather than testing specific learned knowledge or trivia, it measures how well you process information, solve problems, recognize patterns, and use logic. Your final score is a relative measure, meaning your cognitive performance is compared directly to a statistically representative sample of your peers.

What an IQ test measures

Most modern clinical intelligence assessments aim to measure the "g factor," or general intelligence. This is broken down into several core domains of cognitive functioning:

  • Working Memory: The ability to hold and manipulate information in your mind over short periods.
  • Processing Speed: How quickly you can accurately process simple or routine visual information.
  • Fluid Reasoning: The capacity to think logically and solve novel problems without relying on prior knowledge.
  • Verbal Comprehension: The ability to understand, analyze, and use language effectively.

Common question types

To assess these domains, IQ tests employ various puzzle formats. If you take a cognitive assessment, you will likely encounter:

  • Pattern Recognition: Identifying the next shape or sequence in a visual matrix.
  • Numeric Reasoning: Finding the missing number in a mathematical sequence.
  • Logical Deduction: Solving syllogisms (e.g., "All X are Y. Some Y are Z. Are all X Z?").
  • Verbal Analogies: Identifying relationships between words (e.g., "Tree is to Forest as Star is to...").

How IQ scores are usually scaled

Modern IQ tests use a statistical normal distribution known as a bell curve. The mean (average) score of the population is anchored exactly at 100, with a standard deviation of 15 points. This means:

  • 68% of people score between 85 and 115 (Average).
  • 95% of people score between 70 and 130.
  • 98th Percentile: A score of 130 or higher, generally considered the threshold for high-IQ societies like Mensa.

How this site differs

It is important to draw a clear distinction between a clinical assessment and an online self-insight quiz. IQ·Test provides a 16-question online cognitive assessment covering numeric, verbal, logic, and pattern recognition skills. It uses the familiar 85-145 index purely as an entertaining frame of reference. A true clinical assessment, like the WAIS-IV, is administered by a licensed psychologist over several hours and is extensively normed across demographics.

Limits of entertainment and self-insight tests

While online cognitive quizzes are engaging and provide a useful snapshot of your puzzle-solving speed and logical reasoning, they are not diagnostic tools. They should not be used for educational placement, clinical diagnoses, or hiring decisions. They are best enjoyed as a tool for personal reflection and a fun way to exercise your brain.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you prepare for an IQ test?
While you cannot study facts to improve your score, familiarizing yourself with the format of pattern recognition, logic puzzles, and reasoning questions can reduce test anxiety and slightly improve performance by making you faster.
Is an IQ score permanent?
No. Cognitive abilities can shift over a lifetime due to aging, education, health, and neuroplasticity. However, scores taken in adulthood tend to remain relatively stable compared to childhood scores.
