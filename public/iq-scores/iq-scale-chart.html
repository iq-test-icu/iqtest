IQ Scale Chart: Complete Score and Percentile Distribution

Published: August 14, 2026 • Reviewed by: APEX Business Systems Ltd. • Editorial Standards
The IQ scale is calibrated on a standard normal bell curve with a mean of 100 and a standard deviation of 15. The middle 68% of the population scores between 85 and 115, while scores of 130 and above represent the top 2.2% (gifted range). Below is the comprehensive score-to-percentile conversion chart.

The mathematics of the IQ bell curve

Modern intelligence scales use the deviation IQ method, developed by David Wechsler in 1939. Prior to Wechsler, intelligence was calculated as a "ratio IQ" (mental age divided by chronological age multiplied by 100), which broke down in adulthood. Under deviation IQ, your score represents where your raw performance falls relative to the normal distribution of your age peers.

Because the normal distribution is defined by standard mathematical formulas, every IQ score corresponds to an exact percentile rank. A standard deviation (SD) of 15 means:

  • Mean (100): Exactly 50% of the population scores above and 50% below.
  • ±1 SD (85 to 115): Contains 68.26% of the population (the broad average range).
  • ±2 SD (70 to 130): Contains 95.44% of the population.
  • ±3 SD (55 to 145): Contains 99.74% of the population.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the standard deviation on most IQ tests?
Most modern intelligence tests (such as the WAIS-IV and Cattell Culture Fair) use a standard deviation of 15. The Stanford-Binet Fifth Edition also uses SD 15, while older Stanford-Binet Form L-M used SD 16.
What percentage of people have an IQ between 85 and 115?
Exactly 68.26% of the population falls within one standard deviation (85 to 115) on a standard normal distribution curve.
Why do tests stop scoring reliably above 145 or 160?
At extreme scores (+3 to +4 standard deviations), the population sample size becomes so small that question calibration loses statistical reliability.
